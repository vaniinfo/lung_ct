# Let masks measure, let models describe

No open model released so far can be trusted to "look" at a 3D chest CT and correctly say which side, which zone, and whether the disease is peripheral or central. **No paper we found reports a laterality or zone accuracy score for any generative 3D CT model** ([NV-Reason-CT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27511); [MIS-Ground](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14579v2)). So the team's results (side 6/10, zone 3/10) are what the field would predict, not a sign that the setup is broken. The best path is to split the work. Segmentation masks and plain arithmetic answer the "where" questions (side, lobe, zone, peripheral vs central). Classifier models supply probability evidence for "what" (emphysema, effusion, nodules, bronchiectasis). A generative model is then used only as a cross-check "second reader". Published CT agents built this way beat stand-alone vision-language models by about **10–25 accuracy points** or several points of F1. Their gains come mostly from numbers measured off masks, not from the model looking at pictures ([DeepTumorVQA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09679); [3DMedAgent](https://arxiv.org/html/2602.18064v1); [MedScribe](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01779)). We recommend trying four things next: **NV-Reason-CT** as the new generative "eyes"; **EXACT's** anomaly maps as a lesion-locating evidence tool; a **dedicated opacity segmenter combined with the TotalSegmentator lobes** for deterministic laterality and zone; and **COLIPRI** (or OrganLens) as a Windows-friendly abnormality classifier. Almost every candidate was trained on CT-RATE and carries a non-commercial restriction, and several need Linux-only parts (flash-attention, Mamba). Each section below flags these.

## Your three test results match what the published benchmarks predict

Start with the "generative eyes": models that take a CT volume and write text. Most open ones score poorly on chest CT once you check clinical correctness rather than how fluent the writing sounds. One standard test is CT-RATE report generation, where a score of 1.0 means every labelled finding was reported correctly. On that test, CT-CHAT scores **0.184**, RadFM **0.059**, M3D-LaMed **0.148** and BTB3D **0.258** (as tabulated by the [NV-Reason-CT authors](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27511)). The same authors' classification table, covering 18 abnormalities on 3,002 CT-RATE cases, puts **MedGemma 1.5 at a macro-F1 of 0.3033**. That is in the bottom half, close to M3D-LaMed (0.3046) ([NV-Reason-CT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27511)). In EXACT's tables, **Hulu-Med was the best of the older baselines** on the external RAD-ChestCT set, but it still scored only **0.279** ([EXACT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24146)). That fits the team's finding that Hulu-Med-7B missed the three mildest cases. Recall is the deeper problem. On RAD-ChestCT, CT-CHAT catches only **17%** of true findings and RadFM only **4%** ([NV-Reason-CT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27511)).

The "same textbook answer every time" behaviour also has a published explanation. A spatial-grounding study found that 3D medical models lean on anatomical "priors", meaning what is usually true, instead of what is in the image. The study also found they sometimes flip direction words like superior and inferior. **M3D scored 15.9%** and **Med3DVLM 0%** overall. MedGemma models "mostly followed the prompt while still giving poor responses" ([MIS-Ground](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.14579v2)). When questions are well designed, zero-shot 3D models (models tested without extra training on that task) land at coin-flip level. On DeepChestVQA, RadFM scored **0.37** and M3D **0.41**, against a random baseline of **0.40** ([3DMedAgent](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.18064)). The DeepTumorVQA authors conclude flatly that "general volumetric pretraining alone does not transfer to clinical VQA" ([DeepTumorVQA](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.09679)).

A warning about your own scoring (this is our inference, not from a source): COVID is usually bilateral, lower-zone and peripheral. A model that always gives that answer can score well without looking at the image at all. Score every model against a "dumb baseline" that always outputs the most common answer. Also ten cases is too few to separate 6/10 from chance with any confidence.

The VRAM figures are our rule-of-thumb estimates (about 2 bytes per parameter in bf16). No source measured them.

**NV-Reason-CT** (NVIDIA, about September 2026) stands out for three reasons that matter for laterality. First, it reads the whole volume natively in 3D: a 192×192×192 cube at 2 mm spacing, cut into a 24×24×24 grid of patches. Each patch keeps its depth, height and width position, so the model knows where in the cube each patch sits. Second, every scan is turned to a fixed orientation (LPS) before the model sees it, so the patient's left is always on the same side of the cube. Third, its training questions explicitly included **laterality, location and severity** ([NV-Reason-CT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27511)). Its self-reported CT-RATE report F1 of **0.592** is more than three times CT-CHAT's. On the outside RAD-ChestCT set, it reached an AUROC of **0.796** for direct classification ([NV-Reason-CT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27511)). (AUROC measures how well a model ranks sick cases above healthy ones: 0.5 is a coin flip and 1.0 is perfect.)

Treat it with care. All its numbers come from its own authors. It never reports how often it gets laterality right, even though it trained on it. Its writing scored *lower* than CT-CHAT on the GREEN text-quality metric (0.407 vs 0.436). And the authors admit it can state findings the image does not support, and that it misses anything outside its one fixed-size crop ([NV-Reason-CT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27511)). The practical risk is memory. **13,824 visual tokens** per scan, with no merging, is a long sequence. Without flash-attention, standard PyTorch attention (SDPA or "eager") may push a 4B model toward the 24 GB limit. Our research also could not confirm the weights licence or whether the code requires flash-attention.

The other options are weaker or harder to use. MedM-VL-CT-Chest-3B is cheap and Apache-licensed, but it squeezes a chest into **32 slices** ([MedM-VL](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.04323)). Picture a thin-section CT of several hundred slices reduced to 32: faint ground-glass in one lobe can simply disappear. Its 0.924 multiple-choice score also overstates real ability. Free-form findings scores in this field sit around 0.2–0.4. EXACT-CHAT's headline 0.501 depends on a closed **GPT-4.1** cleanup step that calls an outside API ([EXACT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24146)), which conflicts with a local-only setup. Both Hulu-Med and MedM-VL pin `flash-attn` in their installs ([Hulu-Med card](https://huggingface.co/ZJU-AI4H/Hulu-Med-7B); [MedM-VL GitHub](https://github.com/msiip/MedM-VL)). On Windows that means switching the attention setting to SDPA by hand.

## Classifiers and encoders make better evidence tools than chatbots

Classifier and encoder models do not write sentences. They output a probability for each abnormality, such as "emphysema: 0.81". That makes them easy to put in an evidence table and to check. They also score much better than the chatbots on the same labels. The table shows self-reported macro AUROC across about 16–18 CT-RATE abnormalities. RAD-ChestCT is the fairer test because it is an outside hospital's data.

| fVLM | zero-shot | 0.778 | 0.680 | Anatomy level; all lobes merged into one "Lung" | Weights unconfirmed |
| **EXACT** | zero-shot | **0.830** | **0.728** | **18 organ-masked 3D anomaly maps** | Not stated |

Sources: [CT-CLIP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.17834v5), [fVLM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.14548), [EXACT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24146), [OrganLens](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25164), [Uni-Loc](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13330) (COLIPRI-CRM row from Uni-Loc's table), [COLIPRI card](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/colipri/blob/main/model_card.md).

Two patterns stand out. First, every model loses **0.07–0.15 AUROC** when moved from CT-RATE to the outside hospital. Expect a similar drop on your own scans. Second, "zero-shot" on CT-RATE is not really zero-shot for models trained on CT-RATE's training split, because the test scans look like the training scans. Papers also disagree on the same model. fVLM reports CT-CLIP at 0.704 on CT-RATE, while CT-CLIP's own paper says 0.731 ([fVLM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.14548); [CT-CLIP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.17834v5)). So treat a 0.02–0.03 gap between models as noise.

**EXACT** is the most useful of these for the team because it tells you *where*, not just *what*. It produces an anomaly map for each of 18 diseases, multiplied by the matching organ mask. In a zero-shot test on MosMed COVID scans, its maps overlapped real lesions with a Dice of **0.363** (Dice measures overlap: 0 means none, 1 means perfect). CT-CLIP's heatmaps scored essentially zero (**0.004–0.023**) ([EXACT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24146)). A Dice of 0.36 is rough, but it is enough to say "most of the signal is in the left lower lobe" once you intersect the map with TotalSegmentator lobes. None of the papers report this per-lobe step; it is our engineering suggestion. EXACT has catches. Its input is small (64×128×128). It is built on Mamba layers, whose CUDA kernels (`mamba-ssm`, `causal-conv1d`) are notoriously hard to build on native Windows, so plan on WSL2 (our inference; the paper is silent). No licence is stated, and the authors disclose a Chinese patent filing.

Per-disease numbers for the team's specific targets are thin. Pleural effusion is the easiest: OrganLens reports **0.974** AUROC on CT-RATE and **0.938** on RAD-ChestCT ([OrganLens](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25164)). We could not find source-verified per-label CT-RATE or RAD-ChestCT AUROCs for bronchiectasis, emphysema, nodules or opacity. They sit in supplementary tables we could not extract. fVLM's only per-label figures come from its in-house test set: emphysema 75.0, bronchiectasis 81.6, effusion 86.7 ([fVLM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.14548)). For runs on Windows, the pure-PyTorch models (CT-CLIP, COLIPRI, OrganLens, DALE-CT, and Merlin via `pip install merlin-vlm`) are the low-friction choices. Merlin (MIT) is the model least likely to have seen CT-RATE, but it was built for the abdomen ([Merlin](https://huggingface.co/stanfordmimi/Merlin)).

## Masks plus arithmetic answer "which side, which zone" directly

TotalSegmentator already gives the team most of the measuring tools, all under Apache-2.0. These include the five lobes, airways and airway wall, pulmonary arteries and veins, a `lung_nodules` task, and a `pleural_pericard_effusion` task. The effusion task is flagged as trained on smaller datasets and less robust ([TotalSegmentator](https://github.com/wasserth/TotalSegmentator)). The one big gap is opacity. **TotalSegmentator has no COVID, ground-glass or consolidation task** ([TotalSegmentator](https://github.com/wasserth/TotalSegmentator)). VISTA3D covers 127 classes with click-based refinement, but it lists no opacity class either ([VISTA3D](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.05285v2)). So the team needs its own opacity segmenter. The options are a 3D nnU-Net trained on public COVID-19-20 challenge data ([challenge](https://covid-segmentation.grand-challenge.org/); [data](https://zenodo.org/records/4635822)), the 2D Inf-Net ([Inf-Net](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.14133v4)), or a model trained on the team's own expert masks. If the expert masks are training data, they stop being a clean answer key. Keep a separate set of cases out of training for evaluation.

Once you have an opacity mask, the localization questions turn into lookups. **Laterality** is opacity volume in the left lung vs the right. **Cranio-caudal zone** is the share in the upper, middle and lower thirds of each lung, or per lobe. **Peripheral vs central** is the share of opacity within a set distance of the lung's outer edge. The other targets need little or no deep learning. **Emphysema** can be measured as LAA-950: the percentage of lung voxels darker than −950 HU, computed per lobe from existing masks. It is a standard method, though results shift with reconstruction kernel and slice thickness (our note, not verified in this research). **Nodule** count, size and lobe can come from TotalSegmentator's nodule task or the MONAI RetinaNet detector. That detector is Apache-2.0 and scores a mAP of **0.852** on LUNA16, but it reports no FROC ([MONAI bundle](https://huggingface.co/monai-test/lung_nodule_ct_detection)). **Bronchiectasis** clues such as airway size relative to the neighbouring artery can come from the airway-wall and artery masks (our inference).

Published region-aware report models support this approach. Both Reg2RG and MedRegion-CT cut the volume into regions with an outside segmenter. Their gains over models without regions were small: F1 **0.253 vs 0.212** for Reg2RG ([Reg2RG](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2411.15539)), and **0.450 vs 0.435** for MedRegion-CT over M3D ([MedRegion-CT](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23102v1)). Lung and pleura were the *weakest* regions, held back by mask quality ([Reg2RG](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2411.15539)). MedRegion-CT's most transferable idea needs no neural network: compute "count, diameter, and location for nodules, effusion, and cysts" from masks and turn them into text ([MedRegion-CT](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.23102v1)).

## Published CT agents win with measurements, not pictures

Every published 3D CT agent we reviewed supports the team's grounded-agent design. The details also show which parts to trust.

**MedScribe** is the closest match. It uses TotalSegmentator masks to compute **86 descriptors**, including **left/right and front/back fractions split along anatomical midlines**. A plain Llama 3.1-8B text model then reasons over them. It raised CT-RATE macro-F1 to **0.32**, compared with **0.09** for CT-CHAT alone and **0.10** for MedGemma alone. Giving MedGemma the same tools lifted it to 0.31 ([MedScribe](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.01779)). It also shows how this can fail: on the **10% of scans with oversized lung masks, F1 fell 8.9 points**. **RadAgent** wraps CT-CHAT, CT-CLIP and two TotalSegmentator tools (lobes; effusion) behind MCP, with a Qwen3-14B orchestrator, the model that plans and calls the tools. It beat CT-CHAT by **5.8 macro-F1 points**. It also resisted misleading hints 83.7% of the time vs 58.9%. A version with no extra training and the same tools already beat CT-CHAT ([RadAgent](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.15231)).

The DeepTumorVQA study isolates what actually helps. Accurate measurement tools raised Qwen3.5-9B from **38.5% to 48.5%**. Removing the measure tool cut it to **32.3%**. Adding just 10% noise to the measurements cost **10 points** ([DeepTumorVQA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09679)). Crucially, letting a small model look at image crops *hurt*. Meissa-4B dropped to **31.5%** from 38.2%, because models "hallucinated quantities from crops". Swapping expert masks for TotalSegmentator's predicted masks kept **93%** of the gain ([DeepTumorVQA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09679)). That is reassuring for a team that already runs TotalSegmentator. On orchestrator size: MedToolica found models around 14B and up failed far less often than smaller ones ([MedToolica](https://www.mdpi.com/2504-4990/8/6/162)). Even so, MedScribe's 8B model worked, and fine-tuning a 4B model on tool-use examples reached **63.8%** ([DeepTumorVQA](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2605.09679)). One honest gap: **none of these agent papers report a laterality- or lobe-specific accuracy**. The team would be measuring something new.

## Four things to try next, and how each can trip you up

**1. Build the deterministic localization tool first (highest value).** Train or adopt an opacity segmenter (nnU-Net on COVID-19-20, or on part of your expert masks with a held-out set). Intersect it with TotalSegmentator lungs and lobes. Output side, zone and peripheral/central percentages, plus LAA-950, effusion side and volume, and nodule lobe and size, into one evidence table. This is what MedScribe and DeepTumorVQA show works. Add sanity checks before the agent can cite a row: lung volume within a normal range, and lobe volumes that add up. MedScribe's oversized-mask failure shows why.

**2. NV-Reason-CT as the generative second reader.** It is the only open 3D model with fixed orientation, laterality training and a large reported jump in chest-CT accuracy ([NV-Reason-CT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.27511)). Before building on it, check the weights licence. Load it with `attn_implementation="sdpa"` and watch memory at 13,824 tokens. If memory runs out, try 8-bit loading or shorter outputs. Feed it correctly oriented NIfTI files. Use it to flag disagreements with the evidence table, not to overrule it.

**3. EXACT anomaly maps as a "where is the lesion" tool.** Its 18 organ-masked maps and 0.728 external AUROC make it the best documented open option that gives both a probability and a location ([EXACT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.24146)). Run it under WSL2 because of Mamba. Skip the GPT-4.1 refiner. Confirm the licence first (none is stated, and a patent is filed).

**4. COLIPRI (or OrganLens) as the Windows-native classifier.** COLIPRI is MIT-licensed (research use), pure PyTorch, and close to the top on RAD-ChestCT at **0.766** in Uni-Loc's comparison ([Uni-Loc](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13330); [COLIPRI card](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/colipri/blob/main/model_card.md)). It is also the encoder NV-Reason-CT starts from. OrganLens scores slightly higher (0.767), but its licence is unstated ([OrganLens](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25164)). Expect to fit a small probe on top, since COLIPRI's card gives no ready-made label numbers.

For the orchestrator, a 4-bit Qwen3-14B or an 8B text model fits beside these if you run the tools one after another rather than all at once (our estimate). Three cautions apply to everything above. First, **CT-RATE leakage**: CT-CLIP, fVLM, EXACT, COLIPRI, SPECTRE, DALE-CT, ARC-CT and Uni-Loc all trained on the CT-RATE training split ([CT-CLIP](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2403.17834v5); [EXACT](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24146); [Uni-Loc](https://arxiv.org/html/2611.13330); [OrganLens](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.25164); [DALE-CT](https://huggingface.co/Kentucky-Open-Science/DALE-CT-0)). Never test them on CT-RATE training scans; use RAD-ChestCT or your own cases. Second, **licences**: CT-RATE is CC BY-NC-SA ([3D-RAD](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.11147v2)), so treat anything trained on it as non-commercial research-only, whatever the code licence says. Third, **Windows**: Hulu-Med and MedM-VL pin flash-attention, EXACT needs Mamba kernels, and ARC-CT ships as Docker containers. WSL2 removes most of this friction.

## Conclusion

The question has changed from "which model sees the CT best" to "which parts of the answer should a model be allowed to guess." Laterality, zone and distribution are geometry, and geometry is computable from masks with near-zero hallucination risk. Disease presence is where learned models add real value, and classifiers beat chatbots there by a wide margin on the same labels. The generative model's best job is narrower than expected: a second reader that catches what the tools have no coverage for and writes the final prose from cited evidence.

Two open questions will decide how far this goes. One is whether NV-Reason-CT's laterality training actually produces correct sides when tested independently; nobody has published that yet, and the team's 10-case set (expanded, and scored against an "always bilateral" baseline) could be one of the first checks. The other is whether opacity masks are good enough on the mildest cases, because those are exactly where every model so far, Hulu-Med included, has quietly said nothing.
